SLIDE 2 OF 7 • THE PROBLEM & EDUCATIONAL GAP

Why "Discarded Hardware" Is Actually Living Science Heritage

For over 60 years, humanity has sent extraordinary machines across the Solar System. Yet young learners usually only hear about active missions—missing the untold stories of the silent pioneers that paved the way.

THE MISCONCEPTION

  • Off-world hardware is often dismissed as "space junk."
  • In reality, Apollo descent stages served as launchpads, ALSEP mirrors still bounce Earth lasers today, and Dawn orbits Ceres in quarantine to protect astrobiology.

THE ACCESSIBILITY BARRIER

  • NASA’s NSSDCA catalog holds rich historical records, but text-heavy tables rarely spark a young student’s imagination.
  • Students learn best when they step into the mission and experience the engineering trade-offs firsthand.

SLIDE 4 OF 7 • HANDS-ON 2D INSTRUMENT SIMULATIONS

5 Interactive Mini-Games Grounded in Real Physics

Instead of passively reading facts, players operate each piece of hardware in an HTML5 2D Canvas simulator with procedural Web Audio telemetry:

MOON & MARS SURFACE SIMS

  • Mission 1 (Moon): Drive Apollo 15’s electric Lunar Rover around craters to deploy ALSEP nodes, then align the 45° Laser Retroreflector.
  • Mission 2 (Mars): Interactive 2018 Dust Storm Opacity (Tau) slider + Opportunity Hematite "Blueberry" survey with wind-gust solar cleaning.

GEOPHYSICS, FLIGHT & DEEP SPACE

  • Mission 3 (InSight): Tune SEIS wind damping and frequency filters to lock onto S1222a marsquakes and map Mars’s 1,830 km liquid core.
  • Mission 4 (Ingenuity): 2,400 RPM thin-air flight across Jezero Crater.
  • Mission 5 (DSN): Rotate NASA’s 70m dish to decode NEAR, Dawn, Kepler & Pioneer 10.
